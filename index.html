Baker Hughes (NYSE: BKR): Equity Research and DCF Valuation

Equity research internship, Basalt Wealth Inc · May – July 2026 · Final report: July 19, 2026

The question: is Baker Hughes stock worth buying at its July 17, 2026 price of $55.95?

1Research

I read Baker Hughes' annual reports from 2021 to 2025 and compared its two segments: oilfield services (OFSE) and industrial and energy technology (IET). I also studied industry trends, economic drivers, and the Chart Industries deal: $210 per share in cash, about $13.6 billion, with $325 million in expected cost savings.

2Data analysis and DCF

I built horizontal and vertical analyses of the income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement. Then I built a DCF model: revenue starts from company guidance ($27.25 billion for 2026), the adjusted EBITDA margin rises toward 19.7% by 2030, and cash flows are discounted at an 8.3% WACC with 2.75% terminal growth.

3Stock call

The model gave about $62 per share, about 10% above the market price. A Buy needed 15–20% upside, so the recommendation was Hold / Neutral with a positive bias. I left the Chart deal out of the base case and treated it as extra upside, with debt and integration risk.

Fabric Progress Tracker

Desktop app for the production team · Windows and Mac

The need: each fabric order was followed through chats and calls, so it was hard to see which orders were running late.

1Matching the need

The app follows the factory's real steps: buying yarn, dyeing yarn, weaving, dyeing, printing, finishing, and repairs. Each step has a standard number of days with warning and alarm dates. Managers and employees get separate versions, because managers approve changes and employees enter updates.

2Testing

570+ automated test cases check the step rules, dates, permissions, sync conflicts, updates, and backups. I restored real employee backups to make sure no records were lost, and I tracked every issue by priority until it was fixed and tested again.

3In daily use

Installed on company computers: Windows for managers and employees, and Mac for managers. Updates install from inside the app, and data can be backed up and restored.

  1. Staff update a step
  2. Saved on the computer
  3. Synced to the cloud
  4. Seen on other devices

Built with AI coding tools · Flutter · SQLite · Tencent CloudBase

Cost Management System

Desktop app for the manager and finance team · Windows and Mac

The need: each department sent its own Excel file, and costs and income had to be put together by hand.

1Matching the need

I started from the manager's handwritten cost sheet and confirmed every line: 13 cost items, 2 income items, and the formulas for total cost, total income, and surplus. Two Excel templates match how work is split between the fabric team and the Japanese business team. The finance manager adds shipping, payments, and tax refunds in the app.

2Testing

100+ automated test cases, plus test files with known answers. For example, three files must give income of 10,500, cost of 5,500, and a surplus of 5,000. I also tested tricky cases: duplicate files, subtotal rows, two spellings of one customer, and month conflicts. All tests used a separate test ledger, never the company's real data.

3In daily use

Version 0.8 runs on Windows and Mac. It exports Excel reports with monthly, quarterly, and yearly views. The original Excel files stay on the computer, and only the results sync between devices.

  1. Department Excel files
  2. Check and map columns
  3. Pair by customer and product number
  4. Cost, income, and surplus report

Built with AI coding tools · React · Electron · Node.js · SQLite
